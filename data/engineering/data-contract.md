# Data Contract

Producers and consumers need a promise about the shape and meaning of a table. The page first defines data contracts and collects the articles that argue for them, then turns to the implementation options, from JSON Schema to protobuf and a schema registry.

A contract is what turns a table into a product and what makes quality testable. The same notes are in [Data Product](data-product.md) and [Data Quality](data-quality.md).

The definition comes from service design. A data contract is **a formal agreement between a service and a client that abstractly describes the data to be exchanged**. That is, to communicate, the client and the service do not have to share the same types, only the same data contracts. — [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/framework/wcf/feature-details/using-data-contracts)

The argument for bringing that idea into data comes from a series by Chad Sanderson. The following is a set of articles around the topics of data product / contract etc. however the focus is about data contracts and expectations IMO, therefore I place it here. — by Chad Sanderson. It starts with the problem: [the existential threat of data quality](https://dataproducts.substack.com/p/the-existential-threat-of-data-quality), and why the Modern Data Stack can't solve it. Then the missing discipline: [the death of data modeling part 1](https://dataproducts.substack.com/p/the-death-of-data-modeling-pt-1), and why it must be resurrected in the Modern Data Stack. Then the people problem: [data's collaboration problem](https://dataproducts.substack.com/p/datas-collaboration-problem), and why the Modern Data Stack can't scale without fixing it. The answer is the rise of [data contracts](https://dataproducts.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-data-contracts), and why data pipelines don't scale without them. [production grade data products](https://dataproducts.substack.com/p/the-production-grade-data-pipeline) is the production-grade data pipeline, and why tech debt accumulates without contracts. And (finally!) [a guide to data contracts p1](https://dataproducts.substack.com/p/an-engineers-guide-to-data-contracts) is the engineer's guide, implementing data contracts for entities.

## Implementation

A promise needs a format both sides can check, so the next step is choosing how to write the contract down.

The simplest format is [**JSON Schema**](https://json-schema.org/), a vocabulary that allows you to **annotate** and **validate** JSON documents; the [example](https://json-schema.org/learn/miscellaneous-examples.html) page shows it in practice.

The heavier option is protobuf & gRPC. The source is [Protobuf on git](https://github.com/protocolbuffers/protobuf/tree/main/python), the Python part of the repo for Google's data interchange format. [google dev](https://developers.google.com/protocol-buffers) is the Protocol Buffers home page, describing them as language-neutral, platform-neutral extensible mechanisms for serializing structured data. [what is gRPC?](https://grpc.io/docs/what-is-grpc/introduction/) is the introduction to gRPC and protocol buffers, and [what are proto buffers, what do they solve and what are the benefits?](https://developers.google.com/protocol-buffers/docs/overview) is the protobuf overview that answers those questions. For hands-on work, gRPC — [a basic pythonic tutorial](https://grpc.io/docs/languages/python/basics/) is the basic introduction to gRPC in Python, and Protobuf — [a pythonic tutorial](https://developers.google.com/protocol-buffers/docs/pythontutorial) is the Python programmer's introduction to working with protocol buffers.

Community write-ups fill in the why. (good) Intro to gRPC & Protobuf by. [Trevor Kendrick](https://medium.com/@trevor.kendrick?source=post_page-----c21054ef579c--------------------------------) links his Medium page. (good) What are Protocol Buffers and why they are widely used? by [Dineshchandgr](https://medium.com/@dineshchandgr?source=post_page-----cbcb04d378b6--------------------------------) is the second. [Understanding protobufs](https://medium.com/danielpadua/understanding-protocol-buffers-protobuf-a466d8943df8) is the third, by [Daniel Padua Ferreira](https://medium.com/@danielpadua?source=post_page-----a466d8943df8--------------------------------), and it opens with this summary:

> Protocol Buffers (protobuf) is a method of serializing structured data which is particulary useful to communication between services or storing data.
> It was designed by Google early 2001 (but only publicly released in 2008) to be smaller and faster than XML. Protobuf messages are serialized into a [binary wire](https://developers.google.com/protocol-buffers/docs/encoding) format which is very compact and boosts performance.

One more explainer is protobuf what and why? by [Swaminathan Muthuveerappan](https://medium.com/@swamim?source=post_page-----fcb324a64564--------------------------------). Slightly off topic — [how to choose between grpc, graphql, rest](https://ashish-bania.medium.com/the-exhaustive-guide-to-choosing-between-grpc-graphql-and-rest-b7e4fd6d547e) is the exhaustive guide to picking the right API technology among the three, a choice that shapes the technology roadmap for years.
