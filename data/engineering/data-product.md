# Data Product

A platform that nobody adopts is not a product; the work is to treat data as something with users and owners. The page starts with a definition checklist and a framework for a data product, then moves to treating the data platform itself as a product.

A product needs a contract with its consumers and a platform to run on. The same notes are in [Data Contract](data-contract.md) and [Data Platforms](data-platforms.md).

The definition comes as a checklist: the [Definition, phdata.io](https://offers.phdata.io/hubfs/Checklists/Data%20Product%20Definition%20Checklist_D2.pdf) starts by asking whether there is a general description of what you want to do with the data and what the user is looking for. A checklist says what to ask; a framework says who answers. The (good) [A framework for a data product](https://www.datasciencecentral.com/data-product-framework/) is good on accountability, boundaries, contracts, expectations, consumers, and knowledge.

The same thinking applies one level up, to the platform that serves those products. [A meta discussion on a data platform as a product](https://medium.com/stuart-engineering/how-were-building-our-data-platform-as-a-product-f89142b6547f) is one team's account of building their data platform as a product. [Build a data platform as a product](http://web.archive.org/web/20220707112131/https://productcoalition.com/how-to-design-and-build-a-data-platform-as-a-product-d22329ff5a3f), read through its archived copy, is Filipe Rigueiro's breakdown of the challenges, teachable moments, frameworks, and recommendations for delivering a data platform successfully.
